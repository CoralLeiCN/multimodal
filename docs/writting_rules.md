# Writing Rules

## Plain Language and Audience

Use plain language over jargon. Include technical details only when they help explain an idea or the work. Communicate complex concepts clearly and cohesively. Match the level of detail to the background knowledge implied by the user's prompt and context.

## Direct and Natural Wording

Avoid slop words and phrases such as "Bottom Line:", "delve", "foster", "leverage", "it's worth noting", "importantly", "genuinely", "Question? Answer.", and "This isn't about X. It's about Y." Do not use concluding summaries such as "In short:" or "The simplest mental model is:". Avoid hyphenated compound descriptions and adjectives.

State the intended action directly. Do not add what will not be done, what will remain unchanged, or how results will be separated or categorized. Do not introduce an unrequested alternative through contrastive framing such as "X, not Y" or "X—not Y". Avoid invented compound labels such as "exact-head checks" and "editorial-row layouts", vague qualifiers, and canned transitions. Use plain verbs and prepositions to state the actual relationship directly.
